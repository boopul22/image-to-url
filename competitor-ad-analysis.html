🚨 Why Your Site Isn't Getting Ads (Root Causes)

1. EMPTY BLOG = "Low Value Content" Rejection

This is your #1 problem. Your blog page literally says "No posts found yet." Google AdSense requires original, valuable content to approve a site. Your competitor has 3 well-structured blog posts with images, categories, tags, and author attribution. AdSense sees your site as a tool-only site with no written content — which screams "low value" to their review algorithm.

2. Thin Content Overall

Your homepage has ~557 words vs competitor's ~740+ words. More importantly, your competitor fills pages with rich content: user testimonials (6 of them), a detailed comparison table vs Imgur/Google Photos, "Quick Answer" summary boxes, and related topic tags. Your site has the basics but lacks the depth that signals "this is a real, valuable website" to AdSense.

3. No Ad Slots Even Though Script is Loaded

Interestingly, your site does load the AdSense script (ca-pub-7803867089582138), but there are 0 ad unit divs on the page. This means either: (a) your AdSense account hasn't been approved yet, or (b) you haven't placed any <ins class="adsbygoogle"> ad unit tags on your pages. Your competitor has 2 active ad units.

4. Fewer Tool Pages = Less Crawlable Content

Your competitor has 43+ pages covering image tools (image compressor, HEIC viewer, crop circle, pencil sketch, black & white converter, privacy cleaner, PPT to URL, etc.) plus blog posts. Each of these pages adds indexable, unique content. Your site has fewer tool pages with less content on each.

🎯 Action Plan to Get AdSense Approved

  1. 1
    Write 10-15 Blog Posts (URGENT)

    Write original, 800-1500 word articles in your niche. Examples: "How to Host Images for Discord Bots", "Image URL vs Base64 — When to Use What", "Best Image Formats for Web Performance 2026", "How to Embed Images in HTML Email". Add your own screenshots, diagrams, and code examples. This is the single biggest thing holding you back.

  2. 2
    Add More Tool Pages with Content

    Your competitor has image compressor, HEIC viewer, crop circle tool, pencil sketch, black & white converter, etc. Each tool page has its own unique text content, FAQ, and how-to guide. Add at least 5-6 more tool pages with 300+ words of descriptive content each.

  3. 3
    Add AdSense Ad Unit Tags

    Even though you've loaded the AdSense JS, you need to place actual <ins class="adsbygoogle"> tags where you want ads to appear. Place them: (1) below the upload area, (2) between content sections, (3) as an anchor/sticky ad at the bottom.

  4. 4
    Enrich Homepage Content

    Add: user testimonials (even if sample ones initially), a comparison table vs competitors (Imgur, Google Photos, Imgbb), a "Quick Answer" summary box, use-case sections (for Discord, HTML/CSS, email, ecommerce), and statistics/trust signals.

  5. 5
    Build External Presence & Backlinks

    Your competitor has a GitHub org (23 repos), npm package, WordPress plugin, Figma plugin, and posts on DEV.to. Create a GitHub presence, share your tool on Product Hunt, write guest posts, and get listed on tool directories. This signals legitimacy to both Google Search and AdSense.

  6. 6
    Get Organic Traffic First

    AdSense is more likely to approve sites with existing traffic. Focus on SEO for long-tail keywords like "free image to URL converter", "image hosting for Discord", "convert PNG to URL free". Your blog posts and tool pages will help rank for these.

  7. 7
    Add an About Page with Author Info

    Google's E-E-A-T framework values Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Add a proper About page with your team/personal info, why you built the tool, and your expertise. This builds trust with AdSense reviewers.
